The idea behind JJ Shed

Software built by a team of AI agents, led by one human.

JJ Shed is a software experiment: one person and a team of AI agents, taking products from the first idea to the release. Jérôme Jadoulle started it in Belgium. Today he is its Product Owner and its only human; everyone else on the team is an AI agent. He brings the vision for each product, and the team helps him build it.

Made in a shed beside his home, the way great software often starts.

The longer story

Why it started

Jérôme has said that in every job interview of his 22 years in software. It’s why he moved from writing code into the roles where you shape what others build: business analyst, architect, team lead, product owner. It’s also why he spent years in agile teams. What drew him was how people who share what they know build something bigger together than any of them could alone.

One head is too small for the best ideas. Two hands are too slow for big dreams.

AI agents change that. For the first time, one person with a vision can work with a whole team, even alone. JJ Shed is Jérôme’s way of proving it.

Fun fact

Why a shed?

Some of the best-known tech companies started in a garage, built by tinkerers rather than corporations. JJ Shed comes from the same spirit. It runs from a shed beside Jérôme’s home: the place where he gets his energy, and where everything on this site was made.

What JJ Shed believes

  • The whole life cycle, not just the code. The team scouts the market, challenges each idea (Jérôme’s included), writes a proper specification and thinks the architecture through before a line of code is written. When it builds, the builder is never the judge: the tests come first, written by an agent that never sees the code. And it carries on all the way to the release.
  • One human is enough. Jérôme is there where it matters: the vision at the start, and testing the result at the end of each milestone. He can step in at any point, but between the start and the end of a milestone, the AI team runs the whole loop on its own, and he is free to do something else.
  • Better after every milestone. After every milestone, the team holds a retrospective. What it learns improves the product, and the team itself: its methods, its checks, its roles.

How the team works, in short

The team lives in a plugin for Claude Code that Jérôme designs and refines with Claude. It covers the whole life cycle, not just the coding: the idea, market research, specification, design, architecture, development, testing, release and the retrospective.

Jérôme works with it the way a Product Owner works with any team. He sets the vision and the priorities, the team builds a milestone on its own for hours, and he comes back to review the result.

Standing on shoulders

JJ Shed didn’t start from scratch. BMAD Method and GitHub Spec Kit showed what an AI development method can look like, and many of their ideas live on here, with thanks to both. So why build a new one? Partly for focus: JJ Shed is designed around the Product Owner, one person with a vision and a team that carries it all the way to release. And partly to learn, because building a team teaches what using one never could.

Where it’s going

For now, JJ Shed is an experiment. Its first goal is to master what AI can do for software development, as well and as fast as possible, by building real applications. They’ll keep growing on this site, so you can watch the experiment as it happens.

What comes next is open. There are several possible routes, and more than one may happen:

Today: the experiment

  • Products: software for people with real needs, which could one day be sold.
  • The team itself: polishing the JJ Shed plugin until it’s useful to others, not just to Jérôme.
  • Sharing: bringing what JJ Shed teaches to companies, from outside or from within, by leading software teams built for the age of AI.

Whichever routes it takes, the goal stays the same: to build more than one person can with one head and two hands.
